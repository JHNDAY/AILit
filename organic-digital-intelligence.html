2

How each processes information

Each side runs a repeating cycle. The brain predicts and corrects; a language model turns text into numbers and predicts what comes next.

Organic

Massively parallel, analog, and built on prediction.

  1. SenseSignals arrive from the body and the world as electrochemical spikes.
  2. PredictThe brain constantly guesses what it will sense next.
  3. CompareDifferences between the guess and the input become error signals.
  4. UpdateSynapses change. Memory and processing happen in the same place.
Shaping everything above: emotion, attention, hormones, and the body decide what gets processed and what matters.
  • About 86 billion neurons
  • Trillions of synapses
  • Analog

Digital

Statistical pattern processing at enormous scale.

  1. TokenizeText is split into tokens.
  2. EmbedTokens become numbers.
  3. AttendLayers of matrix arithmetic run, and attention weighs which parts of the input matter to each other.
  4. PredictThe model produces the next piece of output, then repeats.
Memory: learned weights are fixed after training. The context window is working memory, and by default nothing carries over afterward.
  • Behavior that looks like reasoning
  • Fixed weights
  • Context window

The two share a loose architectural inspiration, since artificial neural networks were modeled on neurons. But the brain's spiking, chemistry, and embodiment are very different from the math in a transformer.

How each was created

One emerged over billions of years with no designer. The other was designed over about 80 years. The stages below are not drawn to scale.

Organic: evolved

  1. Billions of yearsEvolutionNatural selection was the optimizer, with no designer and no goal beyond survival and reproduction.
  2. A lifetimeDevelopment and learningEach individual grows and learns from experience.
  3. Across generationsCulture and writingLanguage and writing pass knowledge from one generation to the next.
↓ Digital intelligence was trained largely on the written output of human minds.

In a real sense, it is built from the products of organic intelligence.

Digital: designed

  1. 1943First mathematical neuron models
  2. 1958The perceptron
  3. 1980sBackpropagation
  4. 2012Deep learning breakthroughs
  5. 2017The transformer

How a modern model is trained

  1. Predict the next token
  2. then
  3. Measure the error
  4. then
  5. Adjust billions of parameters

This is gradient descent. Further tuning with human feedback then shapes the model's behavior.

How organic intelligence can benefit

The best framing is complementarity: each side covers the other's weaknesses.

You bring

  • The question
  • Judgment
  • Values
  • Meaning and relationships

Together

Human judgment combined with machine breadth and speed.

Digital brings

  • Breadth and recall
  • Speed
  • Pattern-finding
  • Tireless repetition

Six ways to benefit

  • Thinking partner

    Test ideas, ask for counterarguments, and have your reasoning challenged. It works best when you bring the question and the judgment.

  • Tireless tutor

    Get a concept explained five different ways, at your level, as many times as you like.

  • Synthesis and recall

    Pull together large amounts of material, find connections across fields, and get oriented quickly in unfamiliar territory.

  • Drafts and first passes

    Let the machine do the heavy lifting so you can spend your effort on evaluation and refinement.

  • Accessibility

    Transcription, summarization, and translation lower barriers for many people.

  • Cognitive offloading

    Hand routine tasks to the machine and free your attention for meaning, values, and relationships.

Three habits that keep the benefit real

  • Verify factual claimsDigital systems can be fluently wrong.
  • Keep exercising your own thinkingSkills you never use can fade.
  • Keep judgment and values with the humanAI can inform a decision, but the person who lives with the consequences should make it.
