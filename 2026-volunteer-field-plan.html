Prompt

I would like to get involved in this fall's elections for the Democratic Party. I live in Massachusetts, a reliably blue state. I would not mind working for moulton in the primary (I think markey's best days are behind him), but for the general election, I'd like to help a candidate in a swing state. I would prefer to help someone in the senate (bc despite gerrymandering, it seems the dems are likely to take control). I really want Jon Ossoff to keep his seat and for Sherrod Brown to regain his seat and for Troy Jackson to defeat the hand wringing “overly concerned” Susan Collins to be ousted. (Did you know she used to say that no senator should seek more than 2 terms?). So, I'd like to know which of those candidates could use a volunteer from another state? What other candidates aren't I considering but should? What help (other than donations) could I provide? I don't want to go live in another state, even temporarily since I have commitments here. I have done letter writing in the past, but I'm not super keen. I've made telephone calls in the past but found it frustrating. I am a good writer but have my doubts that any candidate needs that. Perhaps you could help me explore that last idea more thoroughly and give me ideas I haven't thought of.

Massachusetts is safe territory. The Senate majority runs through Maine, Ohio, and Georgia — and it's decided by people willing to put in time from anywhere.

How To Help

Filtered against what you've tried, liked, and want to avoid
Try this

Relational / text-relay organizing

Send your own words to your own contacts through a campaign's peer-to-peer texting tool, rather than reading a script live to strangers.

Why it fits: asynchronous, remote, and avoids the live-call friction you've run into before.
Try this

Digital & list / data work

Cleaning voter files, flagging duplicates, tracking local coverage for opposition research. Always needed, entirely remote.

Why it fits: no phone skills required, done on your own schedule.
Try this

Local chapter support

Indivisible or Swing Left chapters "sistered" with a swing-state candidate often need help with admin, comms, or event coordination — remote and low-pressure.

Why it fits: a steady, ongoing role rather than one-off shifts.
Consider

Virtual GOTV phone banks

Remains the highest-impact tactic in the final days before an election, when turnout — not persuasion — is the goal.

Why it's a maybe: you've found calls frustrating before, but GOTV calls are shorter and more transactional than persuasion calls.
Consider

Letters to the editor

Short, local op-eds in Maine, Ohio, or Georgia papers — self-directed, not assigned by a campaign, and read closely by local voters.

Why it's a maybe: plays to a real skill, but shouldn't be your whole plan — one tool among several, not the job.
Consider

Virtual fundraising house parties

Hosting or co-hosting a small-dollar virtual fundraiser for one of these campaigns, using your own network.

Why it's a maybe: different skill set — organizing people, not persuading strangers.
Lower fit

Postcards & letters to voters

Modestly effective — studies show a small turnout bump — but you've done this before and said you're not keen.

Why it's lower fit: effective for others, but you already know it's not for you.
Lower fit

Persuasion phone banking

Cold outreach to undecided voters, working from a script, mid-cycle.

Why it's lower fit: the version of calling you've found most frustrating in the past.
