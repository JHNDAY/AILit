Welcome Back to AILit SIG

The AI Literacy Shared Interest Group is excited to resume sessions this fall at the Sawyer Road location. We're bringing together retired learners, educators, and curious minds to navigate the intersection of artificial intelligence, humanities, and the evolving job market.

This season, we're launching an exciting new framework that positions applied humanities as essential to thriving in an AI-driven economy—a perspective backed by groundbreaking initiatives at major universities, including Brandeis University's ambitious reinvention of liberal arts education.

First Three Sessions: Fall 2026

1
Narrative Prompting Foundations
Learn how storytelling and rhetoric shape effective AI interactions. We'll explore how narrative prompting—the art of structuring requests to AI systems—draws directly from humanities traditions of persuasion, rhetoric, and communication.
2
Immersive Reading & AI Evaluation
Develop skills to critically read, interpret, and evaluate AI-generated content. Using close reading techniques from literary analysis, we'll examine how to spot biases, understand context, and assess reliability in AI outputs.
3
Applied Humanities + AI Competency
Discover why humanities graduates are increasingly valuable in tech and AI roles. We'll examine case studies from major tech companies and Brandeis's revolutionary initiative showing how critical thinking, ethics, and communication trump technical skills.

Why This Matters Now

The job market is shifting. Technical skills—coding, data entry, routine analysis—are increasingly automated. What employers can't automate? The ability to think critically, communicate clearly, understand context, and ask the right questions of AI systems.

Major technology companies have discovered this: Google found that its best managers have soft skills (problem-solving, communication, critical thinking), not STEM expertise. LinkedIn data shows more liberal arts graduates are hired by tech companies than computer science graduates. And now Brandeis and other leading universities are structuring their entire education around this insight.

AILit SIG is teaching these skills to retired learners who have time, wisdom, and curiosity. You're not learning AI to replace your career—you're learning AI to become an expert translator between human values and machine systems. That's never been more valuable.
