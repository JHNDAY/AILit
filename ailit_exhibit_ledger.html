01 · The Claim, Briefly

A reversal, in outline

Several recent pieces argue that AI is inverting the old hierarchy: routine technical work gets automated, while judgment, ethics, and communication — the durable currency of a liberal arts education — become scarcer and more prized. Kept brief on purpose; the real lesson is in panels two and three.

← earlier (~2022) illustrative direction, not a measured projection for liberal arts 2032 →
The one solid number in this story: the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment in the Computer Programmers occupational code to fall roughly 11% between 2022 and 2032 — a real, citable government projection. There is no equivalent government projection showing liberal-arts employment rising; that half of the claim rests on employer surveys, expert predictions, and opinion writing. The chart's green line is a narrative, not a dataset.

03 · Where the Certainty Breaks Down

Four cracks worth probing in discussion

The hard number is narrower than the headline.

The BLS figure covers the specific occupational code "Computer Programmers" — a category already shrinking for reasons that predate generative AI, including offshoring. It is not the same as "software engineer" or "developer" broadly, which BLS projects to keep growing. Citing it as proof that "programming" overall is dying overstates what the statistic says.

The rising side of the story has no equivalent statistic.

Every "liberal arts will rise" claim in this ledger is a prediction, a survey finding, or an executive's opinion — none is a measured employment outcome yet. That doesn't make the claims wrong; it makes them a different kind of claim than the BLS figure, and they shouldn't be weighed as if they were the same.

Several sources have a stake in the answer.

University liberal-arts program blogs, edtech commentators, and magazines with an explicit humanities-defending mission are well represented here. That's not disqualifying — but a claim that liberal arts will save itself, made by people whose institutions depend on that being true, deserves the same scrutiny you'd give a tech company predicting AI will save itself.

This page itself can't fully escape the problem it describes.

An AI selected these sources, paraphrased their claims, and assigned the color-coded confidence ratings above. Those ratings are a judgment call, not a measurement — a more skeptical reader might tag one or two cards differently. Treat the colors as a starting point for debate, not a verdict.

04 · The Closing Argument

Exhibit A is this page

The labor-market question is genuinely interesting, but the more useful exercise for an AI-literacy group is the one you just did: watching an AI gather sources, sort them by how much weight they can bear, and admit where its own chart is illustration rather than evidence.

That sorting — distinguishing a government statistic from an executive's hunch, noticing when a source has a stake in its own conclusion, catching a memorable phrase that isn't actually a finding — is itself a liberal arts skill. It is also exactly the skill an AI cannot fully do for you, no matter how confident its formatting looks.
