Version 1

A named transcript and three bare questions. No audience, no purpose, no frame, no inclusivity instruction. A capable analyst could answer it — but wouldn't know who they were writing for, or why.

Final (V4)

The same three questions, now held inside a clear purpose — a mirror for the cohort — narrated through a warm frame, and built to include every voice in the room, not just the loudest.

A prompt's completeness isn't measured by length — it's measured by how many of these six dimensions it answers: who's reading this, why it exists, what counts as success, whose identity gets reflected, and what lens should color the facts. V1 named a subject and a wish-list of questions; nothing else. Each coaching pass surfaced exactly one missing dimension at a time, because a writer can act on one clear gap more easily than three vague ones. By V4, the original three questions hadn't changed at all — everything around them had. The facts stayed constant. The shape of attention around them did all the work.
