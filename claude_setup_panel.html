PRIV

Privacy

These controls decide what Claude remembers, what it can look up about your past chats, and whether your conversations help train future models.

Model training on your chats

Free, Pro, and Max accounts can choose whether new conversations are used to help train future Claude models. Turning this off keeps your standard 30-day retention window; turning it on extends retention to five years for those chats.

Settings › Privacy

Use incognito chats for sensitive topics

Click the ghost icon on a new chat to start a conversation that's never saved to history, never used for memory, and never used for training — closing it deletes it for good.

New chat › ghost icon

Generate memory from chat history

Claude can build a running summary of your role, preferences, and recurring context so new chats aren't blank slates. If you'd rather each chat start fresh, pause or reset memory here — pausing keeps it, resetting deletes it permanently.

Settings › Capabilities › Memory

Search and reference past chats

Available on paid plans. Lets you ask Claude to pull up something from an earlier conversation. It only searches when you prompt it to — nothing runs in the background.

Settings › Capabilities › Preferences

Worth knowing: even with training off, conversations flagged by safety systems can still be reviewed to enforce policies — so treat Claude like any other place you wouldn't paste a password or a bank account number.

SEC

Security

Claude.ai signs you in through a passwordless link or a linked Google/Apple account rather than a traditional password — so the strongest moves are around that login path and what you connect to it.

Lock down the inbox behind your login

Since Claude sends a secure sign-in link to your email (or routes through Google/Apple sign-in), that account is effectively your master key. Make sure it has its own strong password and two-factor authentication turned on.

Your email provider's security settings

"Stay signed in" on shared devices

On a public or company computer, skip options that keep you logged in. It's a small friction cost that prevents the next person on that machine from picking up your session.

Login screen

Audit connected apps and connectors

Every connector (Google Drive, Slack, Gmail, and similar) sees only what your account already has permission to view — but the list still grows quietly over time. Review it periodically and remove anything you no longer use.

Settings › Connectors

Worth knowing: if you ever suspect the account is compromised, exporting your data first (below) gives you a record before you change credentials or delete anything.

COST

Cost-effective use

Usage limits are shared across claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Desktop, and reset on a rolling five-hour window (plus a weekly cap). A few habits stretch that budget a lot further.

Match the model to the task

Reach for a lighter model on quick lookups, reformatting, or simple edits, and save your heaviest model plus extended thinking for genuinely hard problems. It's the single biggest lever on both speed and usage.

Model picker › top of chat

Put recurring work in a Project

Documents you upload to a project's knowledge are cached — reusing them across questions costs far less than re-pasting the same material into fresh chats.

Projects › new project

Extended thinking & web search, left on by default

Both are token-hungry. Leave them off for everyday writing or simple questions, and switch them on deliberately for the tasks that actually need deeper reasoning or current information.

Tools menu › in chat

Watch the usage dial, not the wall

Your five-hour and weekly bars live in one place — check them before starting long sessions. If you consistently run out, a spending cap on usage credits is often cheaper than jumping a whole plan tier.

Settings › Usage

Worth knowing: a conversation's cost compounds — Claude re-reads everything above your latest message each turn. Starting a new chat for a new topic is often the fastest way to lighten the load.

Before you close this panel

  • Privacy: decide your training preference, and know where the incognito ghost icon lives.
  • Security: your email (or Google/Apple login) is the real front door — protect it accordingly.
  • Cost: pick models deliberately, use Projects for anything recurring, and check Settings › Usage before a long session.
